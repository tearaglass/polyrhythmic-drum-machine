# Prebuild Questions 39–84

## Musical model and terminology

### 39. What does a voice contain?
A **voice means a sound generator**.

A rhythm generator / clock is a separate object.

The first UI may pair one clock with one voice for convenience, but the underlying architecture must not conflate them.

### 40. Should rhythm generators and sound voices be visibly separate?
**Yes.**

They can appear associated initially, but it should be conceptually obvious that:

`CLOCK → triggers/modulates → VOICE`

This becomes important once one clock controls several voices or exists only as modulation.

### 41. What is the smallest unit of time?
There should be **no musical tick resolution** at the core.

Time is continuous and based on the Web Audio timeline.

Internally use:

- absolute audio time in seconds
- normalized phase `0 → 1`

Patterns may introduce discrete event positions later, but the timing engine itself should not be quantized to MIDI-style ticks.

### 42. What is a step?
A **step is an event slot within a pattern**.

It is not a fundamental unit of musical time.

If a pattern contains 7 steps, each step represents one seventh of that pattern's current temporal span unless warped or otherwise modified.

### 43. What is a pulse?
A **pulse is a temporal event generated by a rhythm/pattern system before probability or other gating determines whether an audible trigger occurs.**

Distinguish:

`clock → pulse/event → conditions/probability → trigger → sound`

### 44. What determines a voice's cycle length?
The **rhythm generator**, not the sound voice.

For a raw clock, cycle duration derives from its ratio and reference.

For a later pattern:

`pattern cycle duration = clock subdivision duration × step count`

More complex systems may define explicit spans independently.

### 45. Should ratios automatically reduce?
**No.**

Store both:

- entered ratio
- mathematically reduced ratio

`6:4` and `3:2` share the same rate relationship but may carry different structural/grouping meaning.

The interface can indicate their equivalence without destroying the original representation.

### 46. Direct ratio text entry?
**Yes.**

Typing:

`5:4`

should be a first-class interaction.

Knobs, steppers, and quick-select controls can coexist with direct entry.

### 47. What kinds of ratios?
MVP:

**positive integer ratios.**

Full system:

- arbitrary rational ratios
- continuously variable ratios
- non-integer relationships
- effectively irrational/free relationships

The underlying clock engine should not assume integer-only timing forever.

### 48. Quick-select relationships?
**Yes.**

Useful relationships like:

`2:1`
`3:2`
`4:3`
`5:4`
`5:3`
`7:4`
`7:5`

can appear as quick selections.

Prefer mathematical notation rather than inventing cute names for them.

### 49. How is master tempo expressed?
MVP:

**BPM.**

Later views may expose equivalent:

- Hertz
- milliseconds/seconds
- clock division
- musical duration

BPM remains the normal human-facing master transport unit.

### 50. What time signatures does the system understand?
The core clock engine should **not require a time signature**.

Time signatures can later provide:

- bar display
- external synchronization context
- convenient grouping

But `7:5` should not need to live inside 4/4 or any other meter to exist.

### 51. Is there a global bar or transport cycle?
There is a **global master transport and master clock**, but voice cycles remain independently meaningful.

Do not make a DAW-style bar the ultimate container of everything.

A configurable master/reference cycle can exist for:

- synchronization
- visualization
- recurrence
- grouping

without forcing every rhythm to reset there.

### 52. Display least-common-multiple / recurrence?
**Eventually, yes. Definitely.**

For rationally related clocks, calculate when the selected clocks—or entire system—return to the same phase configuration.

This should become an important part of the instrument.

### 53. Represent very long recurrence periods?
Display useful information such as:

`Full recurrence: 47m 18s`

or:

`2,835 master beats`

For extremely long recurrence:

`> 6 hours`

or another readable approximation.

The visualization should communicate trajectory/progress without implying the musician needs to sit there waiting.

### 54. Reverse phase / negative ratios?
**Reverse-running clocks should eventually be supported.**

Do not use negative ratio notation as the primary representation.

Prefer:

`ratio = 5:4`
`direction = reverse`

This keeps rate and direction conceptually separate.

Not required for MVP.

### 55. Can a rhythm stop while preserving phase?
**Yes.**

This is essentially **FREEZE**.

The clock stops advancing but retains its exact phase, then continues from there when released.

This should eventually be a performance operation.

### 56. Can a clock exist without triggering sound?
**Absolutely. This is foundational.**

A clock may exist solely to:

- modulate synthesis
- modulate another clock
- detect coincidence
- drive visuals
- provide a temporal reference
- trigger external events later

### 57. Can a sound voice trigger manually without affecting its clock?
**Yes.**

Manual audition and performance triggers must not advance rhythm state unless explicitly configured to do so.

### 58. Can one rhythm generator trigger multiple voices?
Architecture: **yes.**

MVP UI: can remain one-to-one initially.

Do not prevent one-to-many routing internally.

### 59. Can several rhythm generators trigger one voice?
Architecture: **yes.**

This is particularly important because the Subharmonicon-style idea of combining temporal sources is musically interesting.

The MVP does not need a general routing UI yet.

### 60. How are simultaneous triggers handled?
When multiple clocks trigger one voice inside an extremely small coincidence window:

- produce one audible trigger
- preserve metadata that several events contributed
- expose the number/strength of coincident sources later

That multiplicity can eventually influence accent or timbre.

Clearly separated events remain separate triggers.

### 61. Separate modes for polyrhythm, polymeter, phase music, asynchronous rhythm?
**No separate engines.**

These should emerge from the same temporal system.

For example:

**Polyrhythm**
different rates over a shared span

**Polymeter**
shared pulse rate + different cycle lengths

**Phase process**
nearly identical rates + continuous phase displacement

**Asynchronous**
independent/free rates

The UI can provide named modes/presets later, but they configure one engine.

### 62. What happens when switching modes?
Where practical, preserve the current audible timing relationship.

But because modes should eventually be configurations rather than separate mathematical universes, there should be relatively little destructive "mode switching."

Avoid a design where selecting a mode mysteriously reinterprets every parameter.

# Transport, tempo, and synchronization

### 63. Required global transport controls
Initial build:

- Play
- Pause
- HARD RESET

First useful version:

- Play
- Pause
- Stop
- Restart
- Tap tempo
- HARD RESET
- SOFT HOME

Record is not part of the initial transport.

### 64. Does Stop reset phase?
Define them differently:

**Pause**
freezes time and preserves phase.

**Stop**
stops transport and returns temporal position to home.

**HARD RESET**
can reset temporal state immediately even while playback is running.

### 65. When does playback begin?
Internal mode:

**immediately when Play is pressed.**

Future external-sync mode may instead obey the appropriate incoming transport boundary.

### 66. Count-in?
**No initial count-in.**

This is a performance instrument, not primarily a recording workstation.

Could be added later if needed.

### 67. BPM range
Initial sensible range:

**20–300 BPM.**

Clock ratios can produce much slower or faster derived rates.

The underlying engine may support a wider range, but the UI should initially keep the master tempo musically manageable.

### 68. How should tempo changes behave?
MVP:

**immediate tempo change with continuous phase.**

The clock should not reset.

Later add an optional **tempo ramp** mode for smooth accelerando/decelerando.

### 69. Must tempo automation preserve phase continuity?
**Yes.**

Changing tempo changes the rate at which phase advances, not the phase itself.

### 70. Tap-tempo behavior
Not needed immediately.

When implemented:

- use several recent taps
- reject obvious accidental intervals
- reset the measurement after a meaningful pause
- favor a robust average rather than allowing one bad tap to wreck the tempo

### 71. What becomes read-only during external sync?
When slaved to an external master:

External source determines:

- master tempo
- master transport
- master beat phase where supported

Users retain control of:

- internal ratios
- clock phase offsets
- drift
- pattern lengths
- synthesis
- modulation
- local transformations

External synchronization should establish the reference clock, not neuter the instrument.

### 72. What happens if external sync disappears?
Preferred future behavior:

**coast at the last stable measured tempo.**

Do not immediately stop the performance.

Show clearly that external synchronization has been lost.

### 73. What happens when sync returns?
Default:

**softly realign.**

Do not suddenly teleport every clock unless the user explicitly requests a hard synchronization.

Eventually offer:

- Soft
- Boundary
- Snap

reconnection behaviors.

### 74. MIDI clock messages?
Not MVP.

When full MIDI synchronization is implemented, support:

- Clock
- Start
- Stop
- Continue

Song Position Pointer is useful but can come afterward if implementation complexity warrants it.

### 75. What should MIDI clock output represent?
**Master transport tempo only.**

Internal polyrhythmic clocks should instead be output as:

- MIDI note events
- pulses
- other control/event data

Do not make MIDI transport clock semantics ambiguous by constantly changing the output clock according to internal rhythmic voices.

### 76. Is Ableton Link required in the browser MVP?
**No.**

Do not add dependency/setup complexity until internal timing is proven.

### 77. What should Ableton Link eventually control?
At minimum:

- tempo
- beat phase

Transport synchronization should be supported as an **optional behavior**, because musicians may want the instrument phase-linked without having another app dictate whether it is running.

### 78. Reserve synchronization from audio/MIDI/OSC/manual events?
**Yes architecturally, not implementation-wise.**

The timing system should eventually accept different reference/event sources.

Possible future sources:

- MIDI note
- MIDI clock
- Ableton Link
- OSC
- audio transient
- manual tap
- another internal clock

Do not implement these yet.

### 79. Should timing continue accurately when the tab is hidden?
**Yes for audio.**

The musical engine must use the audio timeline and continue scheduling correctly while visuals are throttled or paused.

Do not make rhythm depend on `requestAnimationFrame`.

### 80. What happens after computer sleep or audio-device changes?
Do **not** try to fire all events that would theoretically have occurred while the system was asleep.

Preferred behavior:

- suspend/freeze musical time
- preserve state
- recover audio engine
- resume from the preserved musical state

A future external-sync mode may instead rejoin its external reference.

Audio-device changes should attempt graceful engine recovery without destroying the patch.

### 81. Should clocks advance while audio is suspended?
**No.**

Freeze the instrument's musical time.

When audio resumes, continue from the prior audible state instead of jumping hours forward because the laptop lid was closed.

### 82. Is sample-accurate triggering required?
For the browser MVP:

**stable Web Audio scheduling with a short look-ahead is sufficient.**

Do not prematurely build custom sample-accurate DSP infrastructure.

The architecture should leave room for tighter native/plugin timing later.

### 83. Maximum acceptable timing jitter
Goal:

**no perceptible rhythmic jitter and no cumulative drift.**

A useful engineering target is:

- typical event deviation around or below ~2 ms
- avoid excursions above ~5 ms under normal operating conditions

More important than chasing a theoretical browser number is verifying the output objectively in timing tests.

### 84. Should visuals follow the exact audio scheduler?
**Yes.**

Audio timing is authoritative.

The orbital interface should derive its phase from the same audio-time model used by the scheduler.

Never maintain a separate animation-based clock and hope it visually stays synchronized.
